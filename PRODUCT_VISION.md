# Vibenta — Product North Star

## The Vision

Vibenta transforms Boston into a living, personalized discovery experience.

A user describes or discovers the feeling they want through an adaptive, culturally intelligent, multimodal AI experience. As their preferences emerge, the real interactive city transforms visually and its actual recommendations converge toward places and experiences that fit.

The goal is not a conventional recommendation interface, a collection of predefined aesthetic themes, or an AI chatbot attached to a map. The goal is a new way to experience, explore, and discover a city.

## Non-Negotiable Principles

**1. Unique procedural worlds**

Every session should be capable of producing a distinct, coherent visual composition from reusable rendering systems and assets. Visual variety must come from parameterized architectural, material, environmental, atmospheric, and decorative systems—not from a limited menu of hard-coded scenes.

**2. Meaningful city transformation**

The map must change far beyond its color palette. Building facades, windows, roofs, street treatments, lighting, atmosphere, and spatially distributed themed decorations should respond to the evolving vibe. Preserve actual geographic relationships and navigability.

**3. Genuine multimodal intelligence**

Gemini should use recognizable cultural references, photography, music, film, games, and emotionally meaningful questions. The experience should be imaginative, surprising, context-aware, and adaptive—not a fixed questionnaire with different media inserted.

**4. One connected experience**

Quiz inference, visual scene composition, destination ranking, and map interactions must derive from a shared, evolving understanding of the user's intent.

**5. Real-world usefulness**

Recommendations must correspond to real places and experiences. Preserve accurate venue information, accessible interfaces, meaningful student affordability signals, and usable transit navigation.

**6. Artistic ambition**

The experience should feel contemporary, immersive, cinematic, and distinctive. Avoid generic dashboards, sanitized interfaces, superficial color filters, and decorative effects that don't contribute to the experience.

**7. Composability over hard-coding**

Build reusable systems that generalize to new vibes, genres, cuisines, cultural references, and combinations without requiring a developer to create a new scene manually.

**8. Quality must be demonstrated visually and functionally**

Passing tests is necessary but insufficient. Every major visual feature requires inspection on the actual interactive Boston map. Every major technical feature should improve the user's experience.

## Development Rule

Before implementing a feature, establish how it contributes to the full product vision.

After implementation, demonstrate that contribution in the actual user experience.

If a technically successful implementation does not meaningfully advance the original vision, it is not complete.

Do not silently reduce ambitious requirements to simpler substitutes. Clearly identify architectural limitations and pursue solutions that preserve the intended experience.

The hackathon prototype may implement a smaller subset of the complete product, but that subset must be built on an architecture that supports the full vision.
